01 — Foundations

Design Principles

Six convictions that keep every screen on-brand. When a decision is unclear, these win the argument.

① Optimistic Futurism

Design like the future is a vacation, not a dystopia. Upward angles, rising suns, forward motion. Never grim, never cold — the tomorrow we were promised on a 1962 postcard.

② Exotic Escapism

Every interface is a little getaway. Saturated tropical-desert color and playful motifs give even a settings page a sense of somewhere-else. Delight is a feature, not decoration.

③ Bold Geometry

Boomerangs, starbursts, atoms, amoebas, cantilevered angles. Shape carries the brand more than any logo. Confident lines, hard edges, and the occasional swooping curve.

④ Warm, Never Grey

Neutrals are sand and midnight indigo — never corporate grey. Heat lives in the palette. Even shadows lean warm. If it feels sterile, it's wrong.

⑤ Honest Structure

Palm Springs modernism prizes clean planes and clear function. Generous whitespace, strong grids, legible hierarchy. The flourish sits on top of disciplined bones.

⑥ Tactile & Physical

Hard offset shadows, thick outlines, neon glows. Controls look pressable, like enamel signage and chrome trim. Interfaces feel built, not rendered.

Usage ratio — the 60 / 25 / 10 / 5 rule

Keep it from getting carnival-loud: roughly 60% warm neutral (sand or midnight), 25% primary aqua, 10% one supporting warm (gold / coral / terracotta), and 5% a single electric pop (flamingo or lime) for the thing you want clicked.

neutral → primary → support → pop

Contrast & accessibility

Body text is always Midnight Indigo on Sand, or Sand on Deep Space — both clear AA. The bright accents (lime, gold, neon lagoon) are vibrant but low-contrast: use them for fills, borders, and large display type, never for small body text on white. Pair lime and gold with Midnight Indigo text, never white.

03 — Typography

Type System

Five voices, each with a strict job. Discipline here is what keeps the playful palette from tipping into chaos.

Neon Sign
Monoton ATOMIC OASIS

Hero wordmarks and giant numerals only. One per screen, max. Never body text.

Display
Righteous Make Tomorrow Tropical

All headings, buttons, stat labels. The Googie geometric workhorse.

Script Accent
Pacifico aloha & welcome

Tiki flourish for taglines, greetings, decorative captions. Use sparingly — a garnish.

Body
Poppins Clean, geometric, friendly. Carries all reading text and UI labels with quiet confidence.

300–600 weights. The neutral backbone that lets everything else be loud.

Mono / Data
Space Mono CODE · 1962 · STATUS: ONLINE

Labels, metadata, code, timestamps, the all-caps tracked eyebrows. Atomic-age technical feel.

Live, interactive examples — hover, focus, and toggle them. Every control shares the same DNA: thick Midnight outline, hard offset shadow, pill or soft-radius geometry, pop-spring motion.

Buttons

07 — Imagery & Illustration

Imagery Direction

Illustration style

Flat vector with bold outlines and limited palette — think travel-poster silkscreen, not gradient realism. Subjects: palms and monstera, kidney-shaped pools, boomerang coffee tables, sputnik lamps, finned cars, neon motel signs, desert mountains, breeze-block walls, cocktails with paper umbrellas.

Light comes from a low, warm sun. Long shadows. Two-to-four colors per illustration, always pulled from the palette.

Photography

Saturated, sunlit, slightly nostalgic — Kodachrome warmth. Architectural lines, clear blue-green pools, mid-century interiors, desert-and-palm landscapes. Strong directional light, deep shadows, confident negative space.

Avoid: cool/grey color grades, cluttered scenes, corporate stock, drop-shadowed cutouts, gradient meshes, "techy" blue glow.

Texture & background

Use breeze-block lattices, harlequin diamonds, thin starburst confetti, and subtle sunburst rays as low-opacity background texture. Grain is welcome; pure flat gradients on their own feel too digital.

Iconography

Geometric line icons, 2–2.5px stroke, rounded caps, drawn on the same grid as the motifs. Optionally cap key icons with a tiny starburst or atom orbit. Single weight, single accent color per icon — keep them legible at 20px.

08 — Application

Do & Don't

✓ DO
  • Let warm neutrals dominate; treat bright accents as precious.
  • Keep one neon/script flourish per view — a garnish, not the meal.
  • Use thick outlines + hard offset shadows for that enamel-sign tactility.
  • Anchor playful color on a disciplined, generous grid.
  • Pair lime, gold, and coral fills with Midnight Indigo text.
  • Animate with the pop-spring easing — confident, bouncy, brief.
✕ DON'T
  • Don't use every color at once — that's a carnival, not an oasis.
  • Don't set body text in Monoton, Pacifico, or any accent face.
  • Don't put small white text on lime, gold, or neon-lagoon.
  • Don't reach for cold corporate grey or "techy" blue glow.
  • Don't stack three+ motifs in one composition.
  • Don't use soft blurry drop shadows where a hard offset belongs.
